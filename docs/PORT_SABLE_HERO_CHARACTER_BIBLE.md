# PORT SABLE — hero character art and animation production bible

**Purpose:** define the original visual identities and asset contract for the three playable leads and the recurring cast.  
**Status:** pre-production art direction; no final hero models are included in the current repository snapshot.  
**Non-negotiable:** do not substitute the generic CC0 crowd models, primitive meshes or recoloured copies for the protagonists.

## 1. Overall character art direction

Port Sable is contemporary, grounded and culturally mixed. Character design should feel observed rather than costume-designed: people dress for their work, neighbourhood, money, weather and personal habits. Avoid copying the silhouettes or wardrobe of GTA V, GTA VI, Red Dead Redemption 2 or other commercial characters.

The leads should be identifiable:
- in silhouette at medium distance;
- by colour grouping without relying on a UI nameplate;
- by one or two repeatable movement/gesture traits;
- by a recognisable face in a close conversation;
- in both their normal clothing and the special clothing used in major mission sequences.

The art direction is realistic but art-directed for mobile readability. Materials and silhouettes matter more than tiny surface noise. Do not put high-frequency grime, text decals or micro-detail everywhere. Important clothing seams, face shapes and hair silhouettes must survive lower internal resolution.

## 2. Playable leads

### Mara Kade — driver / mechanic

**Age:** 31  
**Home/work anchor:** Kade Motor Works, Old Quay  
**Palette:** worn oxblood, charcoal, warm canvas and small amber workshop details  
**Silhouette:** practical, compact work jacket; clear shoulder and sleeve shape; functional trousers and boots; gloves or workshop accessories when in the garage  
**Face/hair:** short, practical dark hair with a deliberately imperfect fringe; strong brows; expressive eyes; a face that reads concentration and dry humour without exaggerated caricature  
**Wardrobe logic:** her clothes should look repaired and reused, not deliberately distressed by a costume department. One jacket has a visible repair stitch that Juno made years earlier. Driving outfits can add protection and gloves while preserving her silhouette.

**Signature motion language**
- watches moving vehicles with her head before moving her body;
- checks her hands after workshop tasks;
- shifts weight before a driving challenge;
- rarely performs large gestures while speaking;
- when angry, she becomes still rather than louder.

**Facial performance targets**
- focused inspection;
- restrained amusement;
- disbelief at a broken promise;
- hurt she tries to hide from Juno;
- clear relief after a successful drive.

**Animation set**
- Idle, Walk, Run, Sprint, Start, Stop, 90-degree turn, 180-degree turn, short pivot;
- workshop inspect, kneel/stand, wipe hands, pass/receive small object;
- talk-neutral, talk-explain, talk-impatient, listen-sceptical, listen-soften;
- vehicle approach, enter, seated idle, steering idle, exit;
- race-start pose and post-race reaction.

### Rafi Sayegh — nightlife promoter

**Age:** 34  
**Home/work anchor:** Lowlight, The Velvet Mile  
**Palette:** deep teal, warm cream, black and selective copper accents  
**Silhouette:** layered jacket with a long, readable outer line; a slightly more polished shape than the rest of the crew; venue credentials and small accessories that vary by event  
**Face/hair:** expressive eyebrows and a quick, open smile that can disappear when a conversation turns serious; textured dark hair styled with care but not immaculate  
**Wardrobe logic:** Rafi changes clothes more often than Mara or Elias because the room is part of his work. Keep a consistent shape and palette across event outfits so he remains identifiable. Avoid an exaggerated “rich nightclub owner” stereotype.

**Signature motion language**
- gestures with his hands when making a room feel welcome;
- checks a collar or sleeve when nervous;
- moves through crowds as if he remembers everyone's name;
- briefly loses eye contact when he is about to make another promise;
- after a difficult conversation, his body language becomes plain and unperformed.

**Facial performance targets**
- welcoming public smile;
- fast social calculation;
- embarrassment after a lie is exposed;
- genuine attention when Mina speaks;
- private grief for the people who built Tideglass.

**Animation set**
- Idle, Walk, Run, Start, Stop, turns and crowd navigation;
- greet, handshake, wave to performer, cue a stage, calm an argument;
- talk-charm, talk-deflect, talk-sincere, listen-alert, listen-apologetic;
- use phone, check event schedule, receive pass, point out an event space;
- vehicle enter/exit and seated passenger reactions.

### Elias Ward — former House mediator

**Age:** 42  
**Home/world anchor:** Glass Ward meeting rooms and old House locations  
**Palette:** slate, muted navy, grey, warm off-white and a small aged-metal watch detail  
**Silhouette:** well-fitted but not flashy jacket, straight trousers, understated shoes; a shape that communicates careful control without looking like a generic villain suit  
**Face/hair:** salt-and-pepper at the temples, neatly kept hair, lines around the eyes from years of watching people; calm expression that occasionally reveals genuine warmth  
**Wardrobe logic:** Elias dresses as someone who still expects to be taken seriously by institutions. His clothing becomes less controlled over the campaign, but do not turn the change into a simplistic “good clothes to bad clothes” morality code.

**Signature motion language**
- waits a beat before answering;
- adjusts a cuff or watch when choosing his words;
- holds eye contact longer than is comfortable;
- rarely points; uses a small open-hand gesture;
- after admitting a truth, he does not look away to escape the response.

**Facial performance targets**
- measured courtesy;
- quiet disapproval;
- recognition of a past mistake;
- fear of losing control;
- sincere regret without demanding forgiveness.

**Animation set**
- Idle, Walk, Run, Start, Stop, turns and seated meeting idle;
- sit/stand, place folder, listen, examine document as a prop, close folder;
- talk-measured, talk-warning, talk-confession, listen-neutral, listen-regret;
- greet old contact, handshake, leave a meeting;
- vehicle enter/exit and passenger reactions.

## 4. Hero rig and asset contract

The three leads use separate, bespoke hero prefabs. They may share a common skeleton topology for animation retargeting, but they must not share the same mesh, face, hair or wardrobe assets.

Required paths:
- `Assets/ProjectTideline/Characters/Leads/MaraKade.prefab`
- `Assets/ProjectTideline/Characters/Leads/RafiSayegh.prefab`
- `Assets/ProjectTideline/Characters/Leads/EliasWard.prefab`

### Skeleton and animation
- Use a consistent Humanoid-compatible body skeleton for the playable leads so authored locomotion and interaction clips can be reused safely.
- Keep face performance as a separate facial-rig/blendshape layer; body retargeting must not overwrite facial posing.
- Each hero needs a valid Unity Avatar, a RuntimeAnimatorController and a float `MoveSpeed` parameter.
- Use root-motion policy consistently. The current third-person motor is code-driven, so locomotion clips must be in-place unless the motor is deliberately changed and tested.
- Preserve stable bone names and transform paths across LODs where animation bindings are shared.
- Vehicle animation is a separate set of clips; do not reuse a walk cycle as a seated animation.

### Mesh and LOD starting budgets

These are **initial art targets**, not measured Vivo limits. The final budget must be established by Unity Profiler and Android GPU captures.

| Asset tier | Starting target | Required quality |
|---|---:|---|
| LOD0 | 30k–45k triangles | close conversation, face, hair and clothing detail |
| LOD1 | 12k–20k triangles | normal exploration distance; preserve face and silhouette |
| LOD2 | 3k–7k triangles | crowd/long distance; stable silhouette and colour grouping |
| Face textures | 1K starting point | readable eyes, brows, skin response and facial detail |
| Clothing textures | 1K starting point | material separation and distinctive wardrobe features |
| Material slots | 2–4 target | avoid needless material fragmentation |
| Skin weights | 4 influences per vertex maximum | stable deformation and GPU cost |

A lower-end profile should reduce crowd count, far animation frequency, render scale and shadow cost before reducing the lead's face, silhouette or mission-critical animation quality.

### Prefab requirements

Each playable lead root must contain:
- `CharacterController`;
- `ThirdPersonMotor`;
- `ProximityInteractor`;
- `CharacterAnimationDriver`.

Each prefab must also contain:
- at least one animated skinned mesh;
- at least three authored LOD levels;
- a valid Humanoid Avatar;
- an Animator Controller with `MoveSpeed`;
- Idle, Walk, Run and at least one interaction/reaction clip;
- unique primary skinned-mesh assets.

The current `PlayableLeadPrefabAudit` checks the root component contract, valid Avatar/controller/MoveSpeed, clip names, 3+ LOD levels and unique primary mesh paths. The audit does not assess whether the character is attractive, recognisable, emotionally expressive or visually production-ready. Those require human art review and in-game capture tests.

## 5. Animation and dialogue quality gates

Before a lead prefab is accepted:
1. Test locomotion on level ground, slopes, stairs and a full stop.
2. Test a 90-degree and 180-degree turn without foot sliding becoming distracting.
3. Play a complete conversation with at least three emotions and a return to neutral.
4. Verify that body animation and facial expression can play at the same time.
5. Test vehicle enter/idle/exit and camera framing.
6. Compare LOD0/1/2 while the character is moving and while speaking.
7. Run the actual imported clips through the project's animation audit; file presence is not playback proof.

The three protagonists need different idle and conversational performance. Do not give all three the same generic idle loop, same hand gestures and the same walk style.

## 6. Crowd characters versus protagonists

The 48 CC0 pedestrian GLB variants are crowd-pipeline and LOD tests. They can populate background streets where their license and art direction permit. They are not the principal cast and must never be presented as Mara, Rafi or Elias.

Crowd characters need fewer expressions and simplified animation at distance. The protagonists and recurring named characters need authored faces, wardrobes, gestures and specific animation. The visual difference between a hero and crowd NPC should be clear in a close gameplay screenshot.

## 7. Capture and review standard

For each lead, capture:
- front, three-quarter and side silhouette views;
- face close-up in neutral, speaking, amused, worried and angry states;
- full-body idle, walk and run;
- one signature interaction;
- one vehicle interaction;
- all three LODs at a fixed camera distance.

Record Unity version, Android graphics API, render scale, device RAM variant, frame time and memory alongside the captures. A rendered concept image or Blender viewport is not evidence that the model is imported, animated or running in-game.
