# Project Tideline — PORT SABLE: NO FAVOURS LEFT

**Status: New campaign canon.** PORT SABLE: NO FAVOURS LEFT replaces the earlier Vesper Bay / Ari Solis / Ghost Fleet / Last Open Route premise. This is an original crime saga with its own cast, city, factions and mission design. The existing Old Quay Unity scene is only a technical slice; its old dialogue and mission strings still require migration.

## Creative promise

A three-protagonist open-world crime drama about ambition, debt, crew loyalty and the cost of building an empire. The player begins with three capable operators who are cornered by the same underworld institution. They form a crew, earn a reputation, take increasingly consequential scores and ultimately choose what kind of power they will leave behind.

**Central question:** When a system built on debt gives you power, can you use that power without becoming the system?

The protagonists are criminals by choice, not spotless heroes. They can be funny, generous, selfish, frighteningly ambitious and wrong. The story should let players enjoy the fantasy while showing the people and relationships their decisions affect.

## Port Sable

Port Sable is a fictional coastal metropolis built around a deep natural harbour, an elevated freight line and old industrial districts. It avoids generic neon-beach-city styling: sea mist over hot asphalt, stone arcades beside glass towers, working docks beside expensive nightlife, hillside homes above crowded streets, and outer roads opening into salt flats and wetlands.

Districts:
- **Old Quay:** independent garages, market lanes, ferry edge, elevated rail shadows and aging warehouses. This is the first playable district.
- **Southline:** apartment blocks, repair yards, neighbourhood courts and late-night food streets.
- **The Velvet Mile:** hotels, music venues, restaurants, private events and the city's public-facing money.
- **Breaker Island:** shipyards, dry docks, salvage lots and industrial waterfront roads.
- **Crown Heights:** hillside homes, private clubs, old money and new money.
- **Saltline:** outer roads, salt flats, wetlands and the city's informal driving culture.
- **Glass Ward:** finance, media and corporate towers where criminal influence wears a respectable suit.

Each district needs its own silhouette, road layout, material palette, soundscape, signage, vehicle mix and recurring residents. The city must feel alive outside missions.

## The criminal world

### The House
The House is an underworld brokerage institution, not a conventional street gang. For twenty years it has mediated disputes, matched crews to jobs, assigned informal territory agreements and collected a cut from operators who want predictable business. Its public myth is that it prevents constant conflict. Its real power is making everyone dependent on introductions, favours and debt.

Its three rules are: every favour has a price; no crew owns a district alone; and a debt never simply disappears. The rules create stability for some and a cage for others. The House is powerful but not omniscient.

### Factions
- **The House:** brokers, fixers and legacy crews; some want reform, others total control.
- **The Breakers:** a loose waterfront alliance of salvage operators, yard crews and boat people. Personal loyalty matters more than a uniform.
- **The Velvet Circuit:** club owners, event producers, promoters and people who control access to nightlife. Their currency is reputation, invitation and embarrassment.
- **Southline Motors:** a loose car and street-culture network of rival crews, skilled drivers and mechanics. Not every member is a criminal.
- **Crownline Response:** a private security and risk-management company trying to buy the House's influence and convert informal control into a corporate service. It is disciplined and well-funded, but not all-powerful.

Faction identity must be expressed through dialogue, clothing, vehicles, music, locations, mission opportunities and reactions from residents—not just colour-swapped enemies.

## Three playable leads

### Mara Kade — the wheel and the workshop
31. Driver, mechanic, vehicle specialist and co-owner of Kade Motor Works in Old Quay. Mara grew up around repair bays and once had a future in competitive road events. She walked away after a team job went wrong and let someone else carry the blame. Now the House holds the debt attached to the garage property.

- **Want:** own the garage outright and never ask permission to work.
- **Need:** accept that independence is not the same as refusing help.
- **Contradiction:** hates being controlled, but tries to control every detail around her.
- **Gameplay:** driving, vehicle handling, recovery jobs, workshop interactions and high-speed set pieces.
- **Voice:** direct, dry and economical.
- **Arc:** from “I can do this alone” to a leader who shares risk and responsibility.

### Rafi Sayegh — the room and the rumour
34. Nightlife promoter and owner-operator of Lowlight, a music venue built on borrowed money and personal connections. Rafi knows performers, staff, venue owners, local celebrities and people who never appear in photographs. He wants influence, but can turn friendship into a transaction before he notices.

- **Want:** own the cultural centre of the city instead of renting a place in it.
- **Need:** learn that trust is not currency he can spend and replace.
- **Contradiction:** reads everyone except the people who actually know him.
- **Gameplay:** social access, event timing, negotiation, character connections and missions that depend on relationships.
- **Voice:** playful and theatrical when nervous; unusually plain when telling the truth.
- **Arc:** from collecting contacts to building friendships he cannot simply buy back.

### Elias Ward — the rules and the old debt
42. Former House mediator and collector. Elias left seven years ago after a settlement he arranged destroyed a smaller crew's future. He claims he prevented a wider conflict. The truth is that he protected his position and let other people pay. He returns because the House is calling in old obligations.

- **Want:** settle old accounts and leave with his reputation intact.
- **Need:** admit that regret is not repair and forgiveness cannot be demanded.
- **Contradiction:** believes in rules because he knows exactly how to bend them.
- **Gameplay:** strategic choices, faction relationships, negotiation and interpreting conflicting accounts.
- **Voice:** measured, precise and quietly cutting.
- **Arc:** from controlling the crew “for their own good” to accepting that the crew can reject him.

The three leads must have separate lives, relationships and personal missions. Full character switching is a required future feature, not implemented in the current single-player slice. Do not fake it by renaming one GameObject.

## Supporting cast

- **Silas Rook:** chairman of the House, Elias's former mentor. A soft-spoken institutional criminal who mistakes being needed for being right.
- **Mireya Voss:** Crownline executive. Believes corporate centralisation is more efficient than old criminal loyalties; hides human costs behind contracts.
- **Juno Kade:** Mara's aunt and co-owner of the garage. Capable, stubborn and independently motivated; not a helpless relative or mission dispenser.
- **Mina Sato:** Lowlight production manager. Knows the unpaid work behind Rafi's charisma and is tired of cleaning up his promises.
- **Bash Rahal:** harbour mechanic and skipper. Generous, funny and unwilling to be treated as disposable transport.
- **Anika Bose:** persistent investigator. Not a corrupt stock villain; she tracks patterns and the consequences of the crew's choices.
- **Nico Okoye:** gifted Southline driver and rival. Can become an ally, rival or recurring complication depending on the player's conduct.

## The history beneath the first mission

### Tideglass — eight years earlier

The protagonists did not meet by coincidence. Eight years before the campaign, Port Sable's annual waterfront arts-and-motors festival, **Tideglass**, nearly collapsed when its headline sponsor withdrew. Small vendors, performers and contractors were left unpaid. The House stepped in with a rescue arrangement and presented itself as the only group capable of keeping the waterfront economy moving.

The arrangement tied three lives together:

- **Juno Kade** accepted a rescue loan to keep Kade Motor Works open and protect the garage staff. The property became security for a debt she thought could be cleared after one successful season. She kept the detail from Mara because she believed she was protecting her.
- **Rafi** was a young event promoter whose career survived because the House funded the venue opportunity that eventually became Lowlight. He knew the money came from the House. He did not know that the Kade garage had been used as security.
- **Elias** negotiated and signed the settlement. He believed he was preventing dozens of small operators from losing everything. He also knew the terms left the House with leverage over Juno and chose not to challenge them.

Mara and Rafi had been close friends and collaborators before Tideglass. Rafi's career rose; Mara stayed at the garage. Neither understood the full cost of the deal, and both made assumptions that hardened into resentment. Elias later became Rafi's mentor inside the House and the person Mara learned to distrust. Their shared past gives the crew history before the player ever sees them together.

Tideglass must not be revealed through one convenient document. Its truth is fragmented across Juno's memory, Rafi's old event material, Elias's account, vendor recollections and the way the city physically changed after the festival. The player assembles a human history, not a magic-file solution.

### The inciting event — Harbour Exchange

The House's annual **Harbour Exchange** is a private gathering where rival operators renew agreements and settle old obligations. This year, Silas intends to transfer the House's influence into Crownline's corporate network. The three leads are hired separately for different, apparently ordinary roles around the event: Mara is responsible for a high-value vehicle presentation, Rafi is running the headline venue programme, and Elias is asked to witness a settlement.

A public-facing handover collapses. A set of legacy agreements disappears from the event, the House's public account names the three leads as responsible, and their access to the old network is cut off. The incident is a cinematic failure that reveals the House's power to decide whose story becomes official. The three survive because each sees a different part of the same event—and because none of them has the whole truth.

Silas expected their old resentments to stop them cooperating. He underestimated how much they have all lost. Elias knows the House's accusation is staged but initially hides how close he was to the decision. That omission becomes the crew's first internal betrayal, not a late-game surprise invented to shock the player.

## Relationship engine

The story's emotional engine is a triangle, not a generic “found family” label:

- **Mara ↔ Rafi:** old friendship, shared humour and unresolved resentment. Rafi once promised that Lowlight would help the garage; he avoided the conversation when the House's terms became clear. Mara is the only person who can puncture his performance without trying.
- **Rafi ↔ Elias:** former mentor and protégé. Elias taught Rafi how to read a room; Rafi learned to recognise when Elias is withholding something. Each knows the other's public mask.
- **Elias ↔ Mara:** Elias signed the settlement that put the Kade property under House leverage. He did it to save the business in the short term and never admitted the long-term cost. Mara is not obliged to forgive him because he feels guilty.

The crew should be funny together before it is loyal. Banter grows out of shared memory, disagreement and small repeated rituals at the garage. Quiet scenes after missions carry as much story weight as the large scores.

## Antagonist conflict

Silas Rook and Mireya Voss are not interchangeable “big bads.” Silas believes informal criminal order is a brutal but necessary barrier against uncontrolled conflict. He is willing to sacrifice individual operators to preserve the institution he built. Mireya believes the old order is inefficient and wants to replace it with corporate risk management, standardised agreements and a cleaner public image. She is not secretly running every event; her ambition creates pressure that other characters exploit.

Their alliance is conditional. Silas thinks Crownline will preserve the House's authority under a new name. Mireya thinks she is buying a network, not Silas's permission. The campaign's final conflict should emerge from this incompatible understanding, while the protagonists' own choices determine what happens next.

## Campaign spine — 18 core missions

Three independent prologues establish the leads, followed by an eighteen-mission campaign. Each mission needs a playable opening, a distinct gameplay verb, a named-character interaction, a character- or choice-driven complication, optional content, a visible consequence and a memorable closing beat.

### Prologues
- **P1 — TWO MINUTES TO CLOSE (Mara):** a garage dispute turns into a night-driving challenge; Mara must protect her reputation or accept an easy payout that damages a relationship.
- **P2 — LAST SONG, FIRST LIE (Rafi):** Lowlight's comeback night is threatened by a booking dispute and a promise Rafi never intended to keep.
- **P3 — THE OLD TERMS (Elias):** Elias mediates a debt dispute and discovers the House is preparing a major transition.

### Act I — The Entry Fee
1. **THE CUT:** the three leads are separately hired for one settlement job. A staged public handoff collapses, the payout disappears and the House names them responsible. The player sees the incident from all three perspectives.
2. **NO SAFE HOUSE:** the trio regroups at Kade Motor Works, argues over whether to split or fight back, and makes the garage a provisional hub.
3. **RUNNING TAB:** the crew takes its first independent job; a local operator demands a favour instead of payment, creating the first lasting debt or feud.
4. **THE LONG LOOP:** Mara enters a city-wide driving event against Nico Okoye. Winning, protecting a rival or exploiting a weakness changes their relationship.

### Act II — Build a Name
5. **THE GUEST LIST:** Rafi navigates a high-status event where the House quietly negotiates with Crownline. Who he protects or embarrasses changes future contacts.
6. **DRY DOCK:** the Breakers ask the crew to settle a waterfront dispute. Negotiation becomes a multi-stage harbour set piece and the result changes access to Breaker Island.
7. **PORT SABLE: NO FAVOURS LEFT:** Silas offers each lead a different promise. The player must decide whether the crew accepts, pretends to accept or rejects the offer.
8. **A BETTER NUMBER:** Elias recruits a crew for a major score. Crew selection changes dialogue, capability and the complication profile, not just the payout.
9. **ALL THE WAY DOWN:** the first major score moves from an exclusive venue through the city to an industrial location. The player switches between leads to keep separate objectives moving.
10. **FAMILY RATE:** Mara confronts the garage's history and learns why the House could keep her family business on a debt leash. Juno's role at the hub changes.

### Act III — The Price of Power
11. **OLD ACCOUNTS:** Elias faces people harmed by his old settlements. The player can help him make amends, compensate them or refuse to participate; no choice erases the past.
12. **A FRIENDLY FACE:** Rafi's private deal with Crownline becomes public. Mina and the Lowlight staff feel used; the player decides whether Rafi owns the decision or shifts blame.
13. **NO NEUTRAL GROUND:** the House asks the crew to mediate a dispute Silas has allowed to grow. Every available choice benefits one faction and angers another.
14. **THE WRONG SIDE OF THE WATER:** a rival group responds to the crew's growing influence. A familiar contact must choose between old loyalty and a new opportunity.
15. **LAST CALL:** the hub reaches breaking point. Mara wants to protect the garage, Rafi wants to protect Lowlight and Elias wants to settle the old system. Their disagreement is playable.

### Act IV — The Empty Chair
16. **THE AUCTION:** during Harbour Lights, Crownline and the House arrange a public-facing deal. The player moves between Mara's vehicle crisis, Rafi's event world and Elias's confrontation with Silas.
17. **THE LAST COLLECTION:** the crew makes its final move against the House's hold over Port Sable. Mission structure depends on allies, district state and earlier choices.
18. **NO CLEAN EXIT:** the city celebrates Harbour Lights while the House's future is decided. The player makes the final decision through prepared actions and relationships, not a final menu alone.

## Endings

- **THE EMPTY CHAIR:** the crew breaks the House's monopoly and gives independent operators room to choose their own arrangements. The hardest ending; it requires trust across the leads and faction support. The crew does not become saints—it accepts that freedom means losing control.
- **THE NEW HOUSE:** the crew takes Silas's seat and becomes Port Sable's most powerful criminal institution. The garage, Lowlight and safe operations flourish, but the protagonists begin repeating the rules they once hated. This is the power ending, not the “good” ending.
- **CLEAN EXIT:** the crew accepts Crownline's offer, protects a small circle and walks away. They gain money and safety, while people and places that helped them rise are left behind.

No ending is a simple morality score. The player should understand what each lead gains, loses and refuses to admit.

## Score and side-story standards

Every major score needs a character reason beyond money, a preparation phase that introduces people and stakes, crew selection that changes scenes and complications, at least two broad play styles, a middle complication rooted in a character flaw or earlier choice, a lasting outcome and a quiet aftermath scene. Keep crime mechanics fictional, abstract and cinematic rather than instructional.

Side arcs: Kade Motor Works (garage apprentice, customer and Juno's history); Lowlight (three event nights and Mina's trust); Ward's Old Circle (people affected by Elias's choices); Southline Circuit (rivalries and character payoffs); Breaker Island (who speaks for independent operators); Anika's Case (the consequences of the crew's actions).

## Quality bar and production truth

A 10/10 score cannot be promised. It has to be earned through production and blind playtests. Testers should remember the leads and at least six supporting characters, describe the core conflict, identify meaningful choices, notice hub and district changes, and experience distinct mission types.

The current Unity slice has one active player and a mission builder based on retired story strings. It does not implement character switching, the House faction simulation, the full districts, all eighteen missions, cinematic scenes or these endings. The immediate production path is:
1. Replace the Old Quay builder's old dialogue and mission strings with the PORT SABLE: NO FAVOURS LEFT opening slice.
2. Make Mara the first playable lead while preserving the tested movement, camera, vehicle and save systems.
3. Add lead-profile data and character-specific dialogue before full switching.
4. Stage Rafi and Elias as distinct characters in the hub.
5. Prototype real switching with separate transforms, cameras, input ownership and save state.
6. Build one vertical-slice mission for each lead's gameplay identity before expanding the campaign.
7. Add crew selection and multi-approach scores after the core mission loop is stable.
