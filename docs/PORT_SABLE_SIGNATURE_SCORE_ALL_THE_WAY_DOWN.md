# PORT SABLE — signature score: ALL THE WAY DOWN

**Mission type:** first major three-lead score  
**Campaign position:** Mission 9 of the 18-core campaign  
**Status:** authored pre-production design; not yet implemented in Unity  
**Target first-play duration:** 25–35 minutes, excluding optional conversations  
**Primary spaces:** Crown Heights / The Velvet Mile / Old Quay / Breaker Island approach  
**Playable leads:** Mara Kade, Rafi Sayegh and Elias Ward

This is a fictional game mission brief. It describes character stakes, high-level choices and cinematic game beats, not real-world criminal procedures or security-bypass methods.

## 1. Why this mission exists

The first major score must prove that PORT SABLE is more than a driving game with crime dialogue. It must make the player's three protagonists feel genuinely different, make the crew's relationships affect the mission, and produce an aftermath that changes the world.

The target is **TIDEGLASS ONE**, the one-off custom car that headlined the waterfront festival eight years earlier. Kade Motor Works helped restore it, Lowlight hosted its public unveiling, and Elias witnessed the settlement that later gave the House leverage over the original owners. The car has resurfaced as the centrepiece of a private Crown Heights auction during Harbour Lights week.

Crownline is presenting the car as a trophy of its new partnership with the House. The sale is more than a luxury transaction: it will let the two institutions rewrite who gets credit for Tideglass and who owns its remaining value. Juno knows why this car matters, but has avoided telling Mara the entire story.

**The crew's objective:** decide what to do with Tideglass One before the auction closes. The player can recover it for the garage, hand it to the breakaway crews, or use the event to expose the House/Crownline arrangement and accept a less comfortable outcome.

The score begins as a job for money. It becomes a choice about memory, ownership and the kind of crew the protagonists are building.

## 2. Character stakes

### Mara — the car is not just a car
Mara recognises details of the restoration that no other person in the crew notices. She wants to bring the car home because it represents work her family was never properly credited for. Her blind spot is assuming that physical possession will repair what the House did to the garage.

**Mission test:** can she protect the thing she values without treating everyone else as a passenger in her decision?

### Rafi — the event is full of people he knows
Rafi helped make Tideglass famous. The people working the auction remember him as a promoter who could make a room feel alive. He also knows that his early career benefited from the same settlement that put the garage under pressure.

**Mission test:** can he use his social gifts without turning staff, old friends and performers into disposable pieces?

### Elias — the deal has his signature on it
Elias witnessed the original agreement. He knows the auction's public story leaves out the people who paid for Tideglass One's restoration. He can explain parts of the history, but doing so exposes his own responsibility.

**Mission test:** will he tell the truth before it becomes useful to him, or only when he needs the others to follow his plan?

### The crew relationship rule
The protagonists must disagree before they cooperate. The player should hear different readings of the same event, not three interchangeable jokes. Their banter can be funny, but it must come from established history and the current pressure.

## 3. Preparation — a score built from people

The preparation phase is three short playable chapters, not three menu selections. The player completes at least one activity for each lead; optional conversations change which contacts are willing to help.

### Preparation A — MARA: THE ORIGINAL LINE

**Gameplay focus:** vehicle handling, workshop interaction and inspection of a restored hero vehicle.

At Kade Motor Works, Juno identifies the auction car from an old photograph. Mara compares the visible restoration details with the work she remembers from the garage. The player can help Juno prepare a vehicle for the score or challenge her about why she kept the Tideglass history secret.

**Choice:** trust Juno's account for now, or demand that she tell the whole story before the crew commits.

**Effect:** changes Juno's trust and a later garage scene. It does not secretly lock the player out of the campaign.

### Preparation B — RAFI: THE ROOM REMEMBERS

**Gameplay focus:** social navigation, conversation timing, event staging and relationship choices.

At Lowlight, Rafi learns that several people working the auction once worked the Tideglass festival. Mina is not willing to lend the venue's name to another promise Rafi has not thought through. The player helps settle a performer dispute and decides whether Rafi personally owns his old promise.

**Choice:** put Rafi's reputation on the line to protect the staff, or let Mina handle the decision while Rafi keeps the event relationship alive.

**Effect:** changes Mina's trust and the tone of the auction's public-facing sequence.

### Preparation C — ELIAS: THE OLD SIGNATURE

**Gameplay focus:** negotiation, listening and comparing conflicting accounts.

Elias meets an old House contact who remembers the original Tideglass agreement differently. The player can ask direct questions, accept a partial account or leave the meeting without a useful answer. Silas contacts Elias afterwards and asks him to stop revisiting the past.

**Choice:** reveal to Mara and Rafi that Elias witnessed the settlement, or delay that confession until after the score.

**Effect:** changes whether the crew enters the main mission with a shared understanding or an unresolved secret. Delaying the truth makes the midpoint confrontation sharper.

## 4. Crew selection

The player chooses one support contact to accompany the score. The selection changes dialogue, optional objectives and the personal cost of the complication; it does not simply add a numerical bonus.

| Contact | What they bring to the story | Complication they introduce |
|---|---|---|
| **Nico Okoye** | A personal connection to Mara's driving circle and a reason to prove himself | His rivalry with Mara makes him eager to take a risk the crew did not agree to |
| **Mina Sato** | A relationship with Rafi's venue world and a practical view of event labour | She will not cover for Rafi if he breaks another promise |
| **Bash Rahal** | A Breaker Island perspective and a personal stake in the car's history | He will refuse to help if the crew treats the waterfront people as disposable |
| **Nika Vale** | A direct challenge to the House's claim that it speaks for all independent crews | She wants the car to become a symbol of the Cutwater Circle, even if that creates new pressure on its original owners |

No contact is a disposable “class.” Each has an independent reason for joining and can disagree with the leads.

## 5. The main score — seven authored beats

### Beat 1 — THE DISPLAY

The player arrives at the auction through the selected lead's point of view. A wide cinematic shot establishes the car at the centre of the event, with the harbour visible beyond the glass. Guests see a luxury collectible. Mara sees the marks of a restoration she remembers. Rafi sees old Tideglass faces. Elias sees the agreement that made the car's sale possible.

The player has a short free-roam window to speak with the selected support contact and notice small environmental details. Keep exposition optional; the car and the characters should tell most of the story.

### Beat 2 — THREE DIFFERENT READINGS

The player switches between the leads for three short, authored sequences:

- **Mara:** a close inspection makes it clear that the vehicle's history has been presented inaccurately. She recognises a workshop detail and has to decide whether to tell Juno immediately.
- **Rafi:** a former Tideglass performer recognises him and asks why the people who built the festival are absent from the auction's story.
- **Elias:** a House representative repeats a phrase from the original agreement. Elias knows it is incomplete and must decide whether to challenge it in public.

The sequences share one location but use different player verbs. They should not repeat the same conversation with different dialogue.

### Beat 3 — THE AUCTION CHANGES

The auction begins. A last-minute change to the sale reveals that Crownline's public presentation and the House's private promises are not aligned. The car's buyer is not simply the highest bidder: the sale has a personal consequence for the Breakers and for the original Tideglass contributors.

The mission becomes a choice between preserving the crew's immediate advantage and responding to the people whose history created the car.

### Beat 4 — THE PLAN BREAKS FOR A CHARACTER REASON

A support character acts on a promise made during preparation. The exact scene depends on crew selection and prior choices. Nico pushes too far, Mina refuses to repeat Rafi's old pattern, Bash challenges the crew's assumptions, or Nika attempts to claim the car as a movement symbol.

This is not a random alarm or arbitrary failure. It is the consequence of a person doing what the player already knows they believe.

### Beat 5 — THE DESCENT

The mission moves from the upper public event space into the working layers below it. The player experiences the same building as a glamorous venue, a staffed workplace and a service environment. The title **ALL THE WAY DOWN** refers to the city's social layers as much as its physical levels.

The sequence alternates between:
- Rafi managing a public-facing social crisis;
- Elias confronting a House representative over the original agreement;
- Mara taking control of Tideglass One for a cinematic driving section.

Use authored transitions and player-controlled moments. Do not turn the sequence into a long unskippable cutscene.

### Beat 6 — THE CAR, THE CHOICE

The player reaches the car and has to decide what the score is actually for. The mission offers three broad outcomes:

1. **RETURN TO KADE MOTOR WORKS:** bring Tideglass One to the garage. Mara gains the thing she wanted, Juno must finally explain the original settlement, and the crew becomes more visible to the House.
2. **GIVE IT TO THE CUTWATER CIRCLE:** Nika can turn the car into a public symbol of independence. The Circle gains influence, but some original contributors feel their story has been appropriated again.
3. **EXPOSE THE SALE:** the crew gives up the cleanest immediate reward to make the auction's hidden promises public. The House and Crownline lose control of the public narrative, but the car does not become the garage's property.

All three outcomes are valid. None is a perfect moral answer and none erases the crew's earlier decisions.

### Beat 7 — THE DRIVE HOME

End with a playable drive or a short walk back to the relevant hub, depending on the selected outcome. Keep dialogue sparse. Give the player room to absorb what changed.

The crew should not celebrate as if nothing happened. If they return the car, Juno has to face Mara. If they give it to Nika, Bash or Nico may question whether the crew chose a symbol over the people behind it. If they expose the auction, Rafi sees the public reaction and Elias realises that telling the truth does not put him in control of it.

The mission ends on a character image, not a generic cash-count screen.

## 6. Outcome matrix

| State changed | Immediate result | Later campaign use |
|---|---|---|
| Car returned to Kade Motor Works | Garage becomes a visible story hub and Juno must answer for Tideglass | Family Rate, Last Call, finale support |
| Car given to Cutwater Circle | Nika's influence grows, but independent contributors challenge her ownership of the story | The Offer, No Neutral Ground, The Wrong Side of the Water |
| Auction exposed | House/Crownline public relationship is weakened; crew receives less immediate value | A Friendly Face, The Auction, The Last Collection |
| Juno trusted during prep | She volunteers more of the Tideglass history | Family Rate |
| Juno challenged during prep | She is defensive but cannot avoid the truth later | Family Rate |
| Rafi owns his event promise | Mina is more willing to support him | The Guest List, A Friendly Face |
| Rafi shifts blame | Mina keeps a professional distance | The Guest List, A Friendly Face |
| Elias confesses early | Mara and Rafi enter the score with more context, but trust is strained sooner | Old Accounts, Last Call |
| Elias delays the truth | The midpoint confrontation is sharper and more damaging | Old Accounts, Last Call |

The outcome variables should be named and saved in the story-state ledger before this mission is implemented. Do not rely on dialogue text alone to remember a choice.

## 7. Cinematic and audio direction

- **Visual contrast:** the event's polished upper floors should use warm, controlled light and clean reflections. The working layers below should feel practical, worn and acoustically alive. Avoid turning the lower floors into a generic dark warehouse.
- **Vehicle hero shot:** give Tideglass One a distinct silhouette, sound identity, material response and recognisable restoration details. It must not be a random supercar asset with a new paint job.
- **Character close-ups:** hold long enough for a reaction after a meaningful line. Avoid constant rapid camera cuts.
- **Music:** use an original score motif first heard in the Tideglass memory. It changes arrangement when the player descends through the venue, then resolves during the drive home. Do not use or imitate an existing commercial song.
- **Crowd sound:** the auction feels crowded because people have layered conversations and specific reasons to be there, not because hundreds of identical NPCs are rendered at once.

## 8. Performance-aware staging

The mission must remain legible on Android:
- only a small group of full-quality named characters needs to be active close to the player;
- distant event guests can be lower-cost crowd representations;
- the car is the main high-detail vehicle, with distant traffic using lower LODs;
- do not spawn a city-wide police or traffic simulation simply to make a set piece feel large;
- use authored scene transitions to keep the active area bounded;
- keep mission-critical dialogue, objective cues and driving controls readable at the phone's target resolution.

The mobile performance target is a sustained 30 FPS goal, measured on the actual Vivo T2x RAM SKU. This design document is not proof that the current hardware or build can sustain it.

## 9. Research-derived mission rules

The design borrows general production lessons, not Rockstar's characters, maps, scenes or mission scripts:

- Coverage of Rockstar's heist-design interview describes preparation missions with different types of gameplay, plans that use the crew's strengths and a finale where the player's role affects the result. PORT SABLE applies that lesson by giving each lead a distinct preparation chapter and making crew selection change the emotional complication, not only a stat.
- Gamespot's analysis of GTA V's multi-protagonist heist sequences describes perspective changes and player planning choices as part of the appeal. PORT SABLE uses the three leads only when each viewpoint reveals a genuinely different story or gameplay task.
- CD Projekt RED quest director Paweł Sasko's GDC 2023 takeaways, as covered by Game Developer, focus on delivering information, meaningful choices and narrative complexity without wasting production resources. PORT SABLE keeps the main mission understandable while optional conversations carry extra history.
- Ubisoft Toronto's GDC narrative recap recommends authored anchors, characters that survive less-linear gameplay, and a story scope matched to production capacity. PORT SABLE treats Harbour Exchange, All the Way Down, Last Call and Harbour Lights as anchors rather than pretending every street corner needs bespoke cinematic content.

## 10. Acceptance criteria

This mission design is ready for implementation review only when:
- every lead has a distinct playable preparation section;
- the chosen support character changes at least one scene and one consequence;
- the three approaches do not collapse into the same sequence with different dialogue;
- the player can explain why Tideglass One matters to Mara, Rafi and Elias;
- the mid-mission complication grows from an earlier character decision;
- the final choice changes at least one hub, one relationship and one later mission;
- the drive-home aftermath communicates the cost of the choice;
- the mission can be completed without requiring a long exposition dump;
- the mission can run in a bounded streaming cell on Android.

Playtesters must be able to recall the emotional choice after a day, not just the vehicle sequence. If they remember only the car, the mission has failed its story purpose.
